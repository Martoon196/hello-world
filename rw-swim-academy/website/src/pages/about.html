Meet your coach

Ruby Waller

Qualified swimming teacher. Competitive swimmer. Sevenoaks born and bred.

The short version

I'm 18, I live in Sevenoaks, and I've been in the water most days since I was little. I swim for Maidstone Swimming Club, I've raced at the Kent County Championships, at regional and national qualifier meets, and I was selected for the Kent County ASA Development Camp. In 2025 I was a finalist in the Inspirational Youth Awards, which still feels strange to write.

In 2026 I qualified as a swimming teacher. RW Swim Academy is the result.

Why I teach

I remember being the kid who wouldn't put her face in. I remember the group lesson where the teacher was too busy with nine other children to notice I'd figured out how to hide at the back. And I remember the one coach who got in the water with me, made it a game, and changed everything in about twenty minutes.

I want to be that coach for children in Sevenoaks. Not the one who shouts from the side, but the one in the water showing you, because she still does it herself.

How I coach

  • Confidence first. Nothing works while a child is scared. We fix that before anything else.
  • Technique that holds up. I teach the strokes the way they need to be swum at a county final, simplified for a seven-year-old. Bad habits are much harder to unlearn later.
  • Small groups or one to one. Never more than four swimmers. I know each child's name, level and what they were nervous about last week.
  • Honest feedback. You'll get written progress notes. If your child is ready for a club, I'll say so. If they'd be fine in group lessons, I'll say that too.

The training bit

I'm still competing and training towards the Olympic pathway, which means early mornings and a lot of lengths. It also means my teaching hours are set around my training, and they're built into the booking calendar, so a lesson with me won't get cancelled for a gala.

If your child has big swimming dreams, brilliant, I know the road. If they just want to be safe and happy in the water, that's every bit as important to me.
