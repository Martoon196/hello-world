**Sender:** Ruby Waller, RW Swim Academy.

**Assumptions:** taster bookings open to the list on 1 January (48 hours before public on 3 January) **[CONFIRM dates]**; the founding-member cap is 30 swimmers who book a block or programme, not 30 tasters; the free RW cap exists by January **[CONFIRM]**.

## Email 2: What makes RW different
**Job:** plant the two reasons to choose RW over the council pool or a franchise: small numbers and "she's done it". Framework: PAS. Hook: contrarian.

**Subject line options**
1. (Contrarian) your child isn't behind. the class is too big

3. (Self-interest) what {{child_name}} would get with me instead
4. (Story) the lesson I still teach from
5. (How-to) how to spot a lesson that isn't working

**Preview text:** The maths on a council group lesson, and what I do instead.

**Body**

Hi {{first_name}},

A council group lesson: eight to ten children, one teacher, thirty minutes.

Divide it up. About three minutes of actual attention per child, per week. For a nervous five-year-old, three minutes is not enough to feel safe. For a plateaued eight-year-old, it's one correction a term. That's why so many children sit in the same badge group for a year. It's not them. It's the format.

Here is what RW does instead.

**Groups of four, maximum.** Or 1:1. In a group of four, every swimmer gets eyes on them every width. In a 1:1, it's thirty minutes of one adult whose whole job is {{child_name}}.

**One ability level per group.** Nobody is bored, nobody is behind.

**A written plan for every swimmer.** Level assessment at the start, a note to you at week 4 and week 8, a certificate at the end. You will always know what we're working on and why.

**A teacher who has actually done it.** I'm not reading this from a manual. I know what it feels like to be scared of the deep end, and I know what it feels like on the blocks at Kent Counties. Most swim teachers have never raced. I still do. When I tell a nine-year-old that slow is fast, it's because I learned it the hard way.

**And a proper safety net.** Qualified, enhanced DBS, safeguarding trained, insured, and a venue lifeguard on duty at every lesson.

That's it. Small numbers, a plan, someone who's been there.

Ruby

## Email 3: How the free taster works
**Job:** remove the mystery from the first step so booking is easy on 1 January. Framework: FAB (feature, advantage, benefit) walked through in order. Hook: how-to.

**Subject line options**
1. (How-to) how the free taster works, start to finish
2. (Curiosity) the 4 things I write down in {{child_name}}'s taster
3. (Self-interest) 20 minutes, a written level, no charge
4. (Humanity) it's not a test. promise
5. (Command) know this before you book

**Preview text:** Twenty minutes in the water, five with you, one sheet of paper to keep.

**Body**

Hi {{first_name}},

Every swimmer starts with a free taster and assessment. Here's exactly what happens, so there are no surprises on the day.

**Before.** You book a 25-minute slot online. You tell me {{child_name}}'s age and roughly where they are. Turn up ten minutes early with costume on, towel, and goggles that fit.

**In the water (20 minutes).** You sit poolside and watch. I'm looking at four things:
1. How they get in. Ladder, steps, jump, or a sit-and-slide. Tells me their confidence before they've swum a stroke.
2. Face and breath. Can they put their face in and blow out? This decides the programme more than anything.
3. Float and recover. Back float, then feet down on their own. That's the safety skill.
4. One width, any stroke. Then I know what to fix first.

If {{child_name}} doesn't want to get in, that's fine. We'll sit on the edge and put feet in, and that tells me plenty too.

**With you (5 minutes).** I show you a one-page written assessment: their level, what they can do, what we'd work on first, and which programme fits. You keep it whether or not you book.

**After.** I email you the assessment the same day with a recommended block and a booking link. You decide at home, not on poolside. And if you book a block and {{child_name}} doesn't want to come back after the first lesson, tell us and we'll refund the rest.

Six tasters a week so each one gets proper attention. The list gets to book them 48 hours before anyone else.

Ruby

PS: Next email is the one people ask me about most: what founding members actually get, and why it's only 30.

**CTA:** "Save this email. Booking opens to the list on 1 January." Soft link to the taster explainer page.

Founding members are the first 30 swimmers to book a block or programme when we open in January. Here's what that gets {{child_name}}, in full.

**10% off your first block, and every block after it.** Not a launch discount that disappears in March. For as long as you're with RW.

And the honest bit: why only 30?

Because I'm one teacher with a set number of hours a week, and my own training comes first. Thirty swimmers is what I can teach well in Stage 1. Once those places are booked, the offer closes. No extension, no "we've added ten more places". If I say 30, it's 30.

The list gets 48 hours to book tasters before the public on 3 January. That's the head start. The rest is first come, first served.

Ruby
