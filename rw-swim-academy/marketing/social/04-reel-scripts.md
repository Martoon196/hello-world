# 12 Reel and TikTok Scripts (15 to 45 seconds)

All scripts are in Ruby's voice, filmed on a phone, one take where possible. Format for each: hook (first 2 seconds), beats with timings, on-screen text, CTA, caption. Hashtags: pick 8 to 10 from the sets in `01-social-strategy.md`.

**Standing production notes**
- Film vertical, 9:16. Phone at water level for in-water shots; an inexpensive waterproof case is enough.
- Ruby wears the RW cap or hoodie in every to-camera shot so the brand is on screen without a logo bug.
- No children in any script here. Ruby demonstrates. Consented swimmer footage belongs in the proof templates in `03-launch-week-posts.md`.
- Subtitles on, always. Most parents watch on mute at pick-up.
- Keep every script under 45 seconds. If it runs long, cut a beat, not the CTA.
- Anything marked **[CONFIRM]** is a personal detail Ruby must be happy to share and must be true.

Hook types are labelled so the mix stays varied across the month.

## 1. "3 things I wish my first swim teacher had told me"
**Hook (story, 0 to 2s):** Ruby to camera, slightly annoyed: "Three things nobody told me at six."
**Beats**
- 2 to 12s: "One. Slow is fast. I thrashed for years. The fastest swimmers are the calmest ones in the water. Nobody said that to me until I was twelve."
- 12 to 22s: "Two. Breathe out under the water. Not at the surface. If you're blowing out when you turn your head, you've got no time left to breathe in. That's the gasp."
- 22 to 32s: "Three. The deep end stops being scary the day you touch the bottom and come back up. So we do that in week two, holding my hand, not in year four by accident."
- 32 to 36s: "I teach all three in the first block."
**On-screen text:** "1. Slow is fast" / "2. Breathe out UNDER" / "3. Touch the bottom early"
**CTA:** "Save this for your swimmer. Free tasters in bio."
**Caption:** I got there in the end. I'd have got there years quicker if someone had told me these three things at six. So I say them in the first lesson, every time.

## 2. "Why your 6-year-old hates putting their face in"
**Hook (question, 0 to 2s):** Ruby's face half in the water, eyes open, text: "It's not the water."
**Beats**
- 2 to 8s: "Your six-year-old doesn't hate the water. They hate three things: water up the nose, not being able to breathe, and not knowing when it'll end."
- 8 to 15s: "Fix one: hum. Humming pushes air out of the nose, so water can't go in. Try it." (Ruby hums, face in.)
- 15 to 22s: "Fix two: count. 'Face in for three' is a promise. It ends. They can cope with three."
- 22 to 28s: "Fix three: they put it in. Never you. The moment an adult pushes, you've lost a month."
- 28 to 32s: "Bath tonight. Hum, count, their choice."
**On-screen text:** "Hum" / "Count to 3" / "Their choice, never yours"
**CTA:** "Try it tonight and tell me how it went in the comments."
**Caption:** Most children who "won't put their face in" have just never been shown how to keep water out of their nose. Hum. Count. Their choice.

## 3. "What a Kent Counties race actually feels like"
**Hook (curiosity, 0 to 2s):** Freeze on a row of marshalling chairs, text: "Kent Counties. The bit you don't see."
**Beats**
- 2 to 12s: "Twenty minutes in plastic chairs. Headphones in. Nobody speaks. You swim the race in your head about forty times and it's different every time."
- 12 to 22s: "Then the whistle. Then it's just water, the black line, and your coach's voice saying 'long on the pull'."
- 22 to 30s: "You touch. You look at the board. You either learn something or you celebrate. Sometimes both. Sometimes neither and you go and sit in the car." **[CONFIRM Ruby happy to be this honest]**
- 30 to 36s: Ruby poolside: "That's where my teaching comes from. Not a manual."
**On-screen text:** "Marshalling" / "Whistle" / "The board" / "That's what I teach from"
**CTA:** "Follow @rubywaller.swims for race days. @rwswimacademy for lessons."
**Caption:** People ask what county racing feels like. Mostly waiting, then about a minute where everything you practised either shows up or it doesn't. Every lesson I teach starts there.

## 5. "The 1 breaststroke kick mistake"
**Hook (contrarian, 0 to 2s):** Underwater side view of a wide, splayed kick, text: "This kick is a brake."
**Beats**
- 2 to 9s: "Feet turned out too early, knees too wide. It feels powerful. It's actually stopping you."
- 9 to 19s: "Order matters. Heels to bum first. Then turn the feet out. Then whip round and squeeze. Three parts. In that order."
- 19 to 27s: Side by side, same effort: "Wide kick. Narrow kick. Look at the glide."
- 27 to 33s: "Breaststroke is my stroke. Stroke clinics for club swimmers: 60 minutes, video analysis, take-home drills."
**On-screen text:** "1. Heels up" / "2. Feet out" / "3. Whip + squeeze" / "Same effort. Look at the glide."
**CTA:** "Send this to a swim parent. Clinics in bio."
**Caption:** The most common breaststroke fault I see in 8 to 12-year-olds. It feels strong. It isn't. Heels up, feet out, whip. In that order.

## 6. "How to tell if your child has outgrown group lessons"
**Hook (listicle, 0 to 2s):** Ruby holding up four fingers, text: "4 signs they've outgrown the group."
**Beats**
- 2 to 9s: "One. Same badge for two terms. That's not them. That's three minutes of attention a week."
- 9 to 16s: "Two. They can swim a width but won't swim a length. That's a confidence gap. Groups can't fix confidence gaps. Someone has to be next to them."
- 16 to 24s: "Three. The teacher can't tell you what they're working on. With ten children, there's no plan per child. There can't be."
- 24 to 31s: "Four. They're bored because they're the fastest, or they've noticed they're the slowest."
- 31 to 36s: "Any two of those. Time for smaller."
**On-screen text:** "1. Same badge, 2 terms" / "2. Width yes, length no" / "3. No plan" / "4. Bored or behind"
**CTA:** "Free taster tells you where they actually are. Link in bio."
**Caption:** Not a criticism of group teachers. I've helped in those lessons. It's the format. Ten to one can't do what four to one does.

## 7. "Parents ask: is 4 too young?"
**Hook (question, 0 to 2s):** Text "Is 4 too young for lessons?" Ruby shakes head.
**Beats**
- 2 to 10s: "No. But four is too young for a thirty-minute stroke lesson. At four it's water confidence. In without a fuss. Face in. Float on the back. Kick to me."
- 10 to 18s: "That's Splash. Short bursts, games, one adult whose whole job is your child. Not one adult for ten."
- 18 to 24s: "If they can sit through a story at nursery, they can do Splash."
- 24 to 28s: "Four to six. Groups of four. Eight weeks."
**On-screen text:** "Not too young" / "Too young for a stroke lesson" / "Splash: 4 to 6"
**CTA:** "Free taster in bio. They don't have to get in on day one."
**Caption:** Four isn't too young. Four in a group of ten with a teacher on the side is too much. Splash is small, playful, and the goal is a child who is happy in the water and safe near it.

## 8. "What I look for in a free taster"
**Hook (curiosity, 0 to 2s):** Ruby with a clipboard, text: "What I'm actually writing down."
**Beats**
- 2 to 9s: "One. How they get in. Ladder, steps, jump, sit and slide. Tells me their confidence before they've swum a stroke."
- 9 to 17s: "Two. Face and breath. Face in, blow out. That decides the programme more than anything else."
- 17 to 25s: "Three. Float and recover. Back float, feet down on their own. That's the safety skill."
- 25 to 31s: "Four. One width, any stroke. Now I know what to fix first."
- 31 to 38s: "Twenty minutes. Written level. Five minutes with you. Free. Six a week."
**On-screen text:** "1. Getting in" / "2. Face + breath" / "3. Float + recover" / "4. One width"
**CTA:** "Book one. Link in bio."
**Caption:** A taster isn't a test. Nobody passes or fails. I'm watching four things and writing them down so we both know where to start.

## 9. "What I say to a child who's crying at the edge"
**Hook (curiosity, 0 to 2s):** Ruby sat on the edge, feet in, text: "When they're crying at the edge."
**Beats**
- 2 to 10s: "I say nothing about swimming. I ask what they had for breakfast. I ask if they've got a dog. I ask what the dog's called."
- 10 to 19s: "Then: 'You don't have to get in. Can you put one foot in and tell me if it's warm or cold?' One foot is a decision they can make."
- 19 to 27s: "Then two feet. Then sitting. Then we're in. Nobody lifted them. Nobody pushed."
- 27 to 31s: "Twenty minutes later they don't want to get out."
**On-screen text:** "Ask about the dog" / "One foot. Their decision." / "Never lift, never push"
**CTA:** "Save for a nervous swimmer. Splash tasters in bio."
**Caption:** The fastest way to get a scared child in the water is to stop trying to get them in the water. One foot. Their choice.

## 11. "Goggles: the 20-second fitting test"
**Hook (listicle, 0 to 2s):** Ruby with red goggle rings round her eyes, text: "Your child's goggles hurt. Here's the test."
**Beats**
- 2 to 9s: "Take the strap off your child's face. Press the goggles on. Let go. If they stick for a second, they fit. If they drop, they'll leak."
- 9 to 16s: "Then the strap. It should be loose enough to get a finger under. The seal does the work, not the strap. Tight strap is why the eyes hurt."
- 16 to 22s: "And junior size. The adult pair from the kitchen drawer will never seal on a six-year-old's face."
- 22 to 26s: "Fix the goggles, fix half the lesson."
**On-screen text:** "Suction test" / "One finger under the strap" / "Junior size"
**CTA:** "Save this for the next swim shop trip."
**Caption:** Half the "won't put their face in" I've seen is actually "goggles hurt and leak". Suction test, loose strap, junior size.

## 12. "Slow is fast: the drill I do with every 7-year-old"
**Hook (contrarian, 0 to 2s):** Ruby in the water, text: "I make them swim slower. Here's why."
**Beats**
- 2 to 9s: "Every seven-year-old wants to go fast. So they thrash. Arms everywhere, no glide, out of breath at ten metres."
- 9 to 19s: "The drill: swim a width in as few strokes as you can. Count them. Beat your number next time." (Ruby demonstrates: long, slow, gliding front crawl.)
- 19 to 27s: "Fewer strokes means longer glide. Longer glide means less effort. Less effort means they get to 25 metres without stopping. And they think it's a game."
- 27 to 32s: "I learned this at county level. They get it at seven."
**On-screen text:** "Fewest strokes wins" / "Count them" / "Beat your number"
**CTA:** "Try it at your next swim. Free tasters in bio."
**Caption:** I learned stroke counting at county level. My swimmers learn it at seven. Fewer strokes, longer glide, less effort, further swum. And it's a game.
