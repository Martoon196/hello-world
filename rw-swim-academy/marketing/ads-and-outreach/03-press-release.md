## Sevenoaks competitive swimmer, 18, launches swim academy for local children

**Kent county swimmer Ruby Waller opens RW Swim Academy in Sevenoaks, offering 1:1 and small-group lessons with a written plan for every child**

SEVENOAKS, Kent, [date] 2027. An 18-year-old competitive swimmer from Sevenoaks has launched a swim school for local children, promising small groups, a written progress plan for every swimmer, and a teacher who is still racing herself.

Ruby Waller, who swims for Maidstone Swimming Club and has competed at the Kent County Championships and national qualifier meets, opened RW Swim Academy this month. The academy teaches children aged 4 to 14 in 1:1 lessons and in groups of no more than four, in hired lanes at [venue] **[CONFIRM]** and other pools around Sevenoaks.

Waller, who was selected for the Kent County Development Camp and was a finalist in the 2025 Inspirational Youth Awards, qualified as a swimming teacher in 2026 **[CONFIRM award and awarding body]** and says the idea came from her own early experience in the water.

"I was the child who wouldn't put her face in," Waller said. "Then I was the one stuck in a group of ten at the leisure centre, doing the same badge for two terms. It wasn't me and it wasn't the teacher. It was three minutes of attention a week. When I finally had a coach who got in the water next to me and explained one thing properly, everything changed. That's the lesson I want every child round here to get from the start." **[CONFIRM quote]**

Every swimmer at RW Swim Academy begins with a free 20-minute taster and a written level assessment, and receives a progress note at week four and a written report and certificate at the end of each eight-week block. The academy offers three small-group programmes, Splash (water confidence, ages 4 to 6), Stroke (technique, ages 6 to 10) and Sprint (club preparation, ages 9 to 14), alongside private lessons, holiday intensives and stroke clinics for club swimmers.

Waller is training towards the Olympic pathway and continues to train with her club most mornings, teaching after school and on Saturday mornings.

"Most swimming teachers have never raced," she said. "That's not a criticism. But when I tell a seven-year-old that slow is fast, or that breathing out under the water is the whole game, it's because those are the things that made the difference for me at county level, and I can see it at 25 metres when it's missing." **[CONFIRM quote]**

The academy is a family business. Ruby's father, Chris Waller **[CONFIRM name and role]**, handles bookings and administration.

"Ruby's 18, and we think that's a strength," Chris Waller said. "Children listen to her. Parents of nervous swimmers, and particularly of young girls, tell us their daughters want to be like her. She's fully qualified, DBS checked, safeguarding trained and insured, and there's a lifeguard on duty at every lesson. We've set it up so that if a child doesn't want to come back after the first lesson of a block, we refund the rest. We'd rather families felt safe trying it." **[CONFIRM quote]**

The first 30 swimmers to book a block are being offered founding-member terms, including a discount on every block and priority booking each term. The academy says the cap reflects the number of children one teacher can teach well while continuing to train.

Free tasters are limited to six a week and can be booked at rwswimacademy.co.uk.
