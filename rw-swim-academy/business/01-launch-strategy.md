## 1. The business in one paragraph

RW Swim Academy is private swimming tuition in Sevenoaks and the surrounding villages, taught by Ruby Waller: 18, a fully qualified swimming teacher **[CONFIRM exact award and awarding body]**, still competing at county, regional and national qualifier level, and training towards the Olympic pathway. Ruby teaches in hired lanes at local pools. The product is visible progress in small numbers: 1:1, 2:1 and groups of no more than four. The first goal is 30 paying swimmers. Nothing about investors, facilities or building a pool is discussed until that number is real.

### The niche

We are not competing with council group lessons on price. We are the next step for parents whose child has stalled in a group of ten, or who is nervous, or who is on a club track and wants technique from someone who has raced. The niche is **small-number, progress-tracked lessons from a young competitive swimmer children look up to**.

### Positioning

*Learn from a swimmer who's been where your child wants to go.*

Three reasons parents choose Ruby, in the order they matter to each customer type:

| Customer type (see brief section 3) | First reason | Second reason | Third reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confidence parents (60%) | Warm, young coach children relate to | Small numbers and visible, written progress | Qualified, DBS-checked, insured |
| Club-track parents (30%) | She has actually competed at county, regional and national qualifier level | Technique focus, video analysis | Pathway knowledge from someone living it |
| Pairs and adults (10%) | Value of a shared lesson | Flexible, local | Same safety and progress standards |

### What we deliberately are not

- Not a "swim school" in the leisure-centre sense (ten children, one teacher, a badge every two terms).
- Not a club. Ruby's own club affiliation is separate. Stroke Clinics complement club coaching; they do not replace it.
- Not a facility. We hire lanes. That is a feature at this stage, not a compromise.

## 2. Why "demand first, pool later"

Chris will have heard, and may have said, "Sevenoaks needs another pool". It may be true. It is not a business plan yet. The order matters:

| Building or leasing a pool first | Hiring lanes first |
|---|---|
| Capital cost of a small teaching pool (4 lanes, 20 m) runs to seven figures once planning, plant and building are included **[CONFIRM with a quantity surveyor when the time comes]** | Capital cost: a few hundred pounds of kit |
| Fixed monthly cost whether or not anyone books (energy, water treatment, staff, rates) | Cost only when a lesson is booked (lane hire is a variable cost) |
| Ruby's time is spent on operations and fundraising, not coaching | Ruby's time is spent coaching, which is the thing customers pay for |
| Unknown demand, unknown retention, unknown prices the market will bear | Every one of those becomes known within two terms |
| An investor is asked to back an idea and a 18-year-old's talent | An investor is shown a waiting list, a retention rate and a coach-training system, then asked to solve a capacity problem |

The lane-hire model also has a hidden benefit: it forces us to find out which venues, times and formats parents will actually buy. That knowledge is what a facility plan is built from.

**The rule:** no conversation about premises until Stage 4 gates are met (section 4). If someone offers a pool earlier, the answer is "show us the numbers on the hire rate and we'll run it as a venue".

## 3. The first 30 swimmers

### Why 30

- 30 swimmers at the launch mix (section 3.2) is roughly 10 to 11 coaching hours a week, which is achievable alongside Ruby's own training (she has about 20 available hours a week in term time: Mon to Fri 4 to 7 pm plus Saturday 8 am to 1 pm).
- 30 is enough to measure retention and conversion honestly. Five swimmers tells you nothing; 30 tells you a lot.
- 30 founding members is the whole of offer 6 in the brief. When it is full, it is full, which is the only honest form of urgency we have.

### 3.1 The funnel to 30 (planning assumptions, not promises)

| Step | Assumption | Number |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries (form, DM, WhatsApp) across Oct to Dec | From local Facebook groups, school gate, Ruby's Instagram, referrals | 80 |
| Tasters booked | 60% of enquiries | 48 |
| Tasters attended (max 6 a week from week 5) | 85% attendance | 40 |
| Taster to paid block or programme | 55% | 22 |
| Direct bookings without a taster (club-track parents, referrals, 2:1 pairs joining a sibling) | | 8 |
| **Paying swimmers by mid-January** | | **30** |

If taster-to-block conversion is below 35% after the first 20 tasters, something in the taster itself needs fixing before more marketing money is spent (see `05-90-day-launch-plan.md`, KPI dashboard).

### 3.2 What 30 looks like (assumed launch mix)

| Format | Swimmers | Weekly pool time |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1 (30 min blocks) | 14 | 7.0 hrs |
| 2:1 pairs (30 min) | 8 (4 pairs) | 2.0 hrs |
| RW Progress Programme (2 groups of 4, 45 min) | 8 | 1.5 hrs |
| **Total** | **30** | **10.5 hrs** |

Stroke Clinics and Holiday Intensives sit on top, mostly in holidays.

## 4. The stage plan, with gates

The brief adopts four stages. Below, each stage has an aim, the work, numeric gates to move on, and the signals that mean stop and fix.

### Stage 1: Prove it (now to about 3 months, i.e. to end of January 2027)

**Aim:** Ruby plus hired lanes; 30 paying swimmers; every process written down once.

| Ruby does | Chris does |
|---|---|
| Teaches every lesson personally | Venue agreements, company set-up, insurance, money, bookings |
| Writes the level assessment for every swimmer | Runs the KPI sheet every Sunday |
| Sends the week 4 and week 8 progress notes | Handles every parent conversation that is not about swimming |
| Films short clips (with consent) for socials | Collects reviews and testimonials |
| Keeps her training and competition calendar sacred | Says no to anything that is not on the 90-day plan |

**Move to Stage 2 when ALL of these are true for 4 consecutive weeks:**

| Gate | Target |
|---|---|
| Active paying swimmers | 25 or more |
| Block or programme renewal rate (swimmers who rebook at the end of a block) | 80% or more |
| Waiting list (named children with a parent's contact, wanting a slot we cannot give) | 15 or more |
| Ruby's coaching hours per week | 12 or more |
| Taster to paid conversion (rolling) | 50% or more |
| Google reviews | 10 or more, average 4.8 or above |
| Monthly gross contribution (see `03`) | £1,500 or more for 2 consecutive months |
| Venue | Written agreement covering at least 8 hrs/week for the following term |

**Stop and fix signals:**

- Taster to paid below 35% after 20 tasters: the taster or the follow-up is broken, not the marketing.
- Renewal below 60%: the progress notes are not landing, or slots are inconvenient, or lesson quality is inconsistent. Ask every leaver why, in person.
- No venue will hire lanes at 4 to 7 pm on weekdays: pivot to school pools, hotel pools and Saturday mornings before spending on marketing.
- Ruby's training times slipping or her coach raising it: cut hours, not training. The competitive record is the brand.
- Chris spending more than 10 hours a week on admin: a system is missing (booking, payments or messaging), fix the system before adding swimmers.

### Stage 2: Add capacity (about months 3 to 9, February to August 2027)

**Aim:** More sessions, second and third venue slots, first additional coach when Ruby is at capacity. Ruby remains the face and the standard.

| Gate to Stage 3 | Target |
|---|---|
| Active swimmers | 60 or more |
| Coaches other than Ruby | 1 to 2, each with renewal rate 75% or more on their own blocks |
| Coaching hours delivered per week (all coaches) | 25 or more |
| Waiting list | 30 or more |
| Monthly revenue | £6,000 or more for 3 consecutive months |
| RW Method | Version 1 written and used to onboard the first coach |
| Retention across a full term change (Easter to summer) | 70% or more |

**Stop and fix signals:** a coach's renewal rate 20 points below Ruby's (quality gap); a venue withdrawing hours at short notice (single-venue dependence); Holiday Intensives not selling out (pricing or promotion timing).

### Stage 3: Multiply (about months 9 to 18)

**Aim:** Second venue running weekly, the RW Method fully documented, coach pipeline of 3 or more, a community/CIC arm considered (section 7).

| Gate to Stage 4 | Target |
|---|---|
| Active swimmers | 150 or more across 2 or more venues |
| 12 months of accounts | Profitable after paying all coaches and a fair salary for Ruby |
| Persistent waiting list | 50 or more, and turning away 20 or more enquiries a month for lack of pool time |
| Year-on-year retention | 70% or more |
| Coaches trained on the RW Method | 3 or more, with written onboarding records |
| Binding constraint | Pool time, provably (not marketing, not coaches) |

### Stage 4: The facility conversation (18 months or more)

Only now does anyone open a spreadsheet about a dedicated pool, and only with the evidence in section 8. The options at that point range from a long-term lease on an under-used school or hotel pool, to a partnership with a facility operator, to a build. The evidence decides, not the ambition.

## 5. Ruby first, then coaches

### The principle

Ruby is the product. Parents are buying her: her manner with children, her competitive credibility and her standards. A second coach is not added because the business wants to grow; a second coach is added because Ruby is at 12 or more hours a week and the waiting list is 15 or more, and there is written material (the RW Method) for the coach to teach from.

Three things follow:

1. **Ruby teaches every founding member personally.** No exceptions in Stage 1.
2. **Nobody else teaches under the RW name until they have been trained on the RW Method and observed by Ruby.** Parents are told in advance and can decline.
3. **Ruby's own training comes first.** The calendar blocks it out. Competition weekends are published to parents at the start of every block, and blocks are dated to skip them.

### The competition-weekend problem (deal with it now)

Kent County Championships and regional qualifiers typically fall on weekends in the spring and early summer **[CONFIRM Ruby's 2026/27 competition calendar with her club coach]**. Saturday 8 am to 1 pm is prime lesson time. The fix is simple: every block is sold with the six or eight dated lesson days printed on it, competition weekends already skipped. A "block of 6" may span 7 or 8 calendar weeks. Parents accept dates; they do not accept surprises.

### Identifying 2 to 3 backup instructors

Do this in Stage 1 even though they will not teach until Stage 2. Backup is needed for illness, competitions and injury.

**Where to look**

| Source | Why it works | How to approach |
|---|---|---|
| Ruby's club teammates aged 17 plus who hold a Level 2 teaching award (or Level 1 plus working towards Level 2) | Same ethos, same standards, same "she's actually done it" credibility | Ruby asks directly; be clear it is paid, occasional, and starts with shadowing |
| Level 2 teachers already employed part-time at leisure centres in Sevenoaks, Tonbridge or Tunbridge Wells who want extra evening or Saturday hours | Already qualified, DBS-checked, experienced with beginners | Word of mouth, and a plain advert on Swim England / STA job boards and local Facebook groups **[CONFIRM no exclusivity clause in their existing contract]** |
| University students from the area home at Christmas, Easter and summer, with teaching awards | Perfect for Holiday Intensives, which is when Ruby is also busiest | Ask club parents; ask the club welfare officer |
| Former club coaches or retired teachers | Experience, gravitas, and often a rescue award already | Ask the club head coach for names |

**Minimum standard before anyone teaches under the RW name** (all evidenced and copied to file):

- Level 2 swimming teaching award (Swim England / Institute of Swimming, or STA) **[CONFIRM which awards the insurer accepts]**
- Enhanced DBS with children's barred list check, ideally on the DBS Update Service
- Safeguarding training in date
- Rescue award in date (NRASTC or NPLQ) or venue lifeguard confirmed
- Own insurance, or named on the RW policy **[CONFIRM with insurer]**
- Signed RW coach agreement and code of conduct

**Onboarding path (about 6 hours of Ruby's time per coach):** shadow 2 sessions, co-teach 2 sessions, teach 1 session observed by Ruby, then parent feedback collected after their first block. Recorded on the RW Method coach checklist (section 6).

**Pay and status:** budget £20 to £30 per teaching hour **[CONFIRM local going rate; Kent leisure centres pay roughly £15 to £25 per hour for Level 2 teachers]**. Whether a coach is self-employed or an employee depends on control, fixed hours and substitution rights; get an accountant's view before the first coach starts, because getting it wrong creates a PAYE liability.

## 6. The RW Method

"The RW Method" is what makes the business worth more than Ruby's diary. It is the written system that lets another coach deliver the same lesson, the same progress note and the same parent experience. It is also the single most important document for Stage 3 and for any investor.

### What to document from day one (Stage 1 habit, not a Stage 3 project)

| Document | Started when | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Level criteria: Splash, Stroke, Sprint, each with 5 to 8 observable skills (template in `06`) | Before the first taster | Ruby |
| The taster and assessment script (what Ruby does in 20 minutes, what she says to the parent in 5) | Before the first taster | Ruby, Chris edits |
| Lesson structure (warm-up, skill focus, apply and play, feedback) with timings for 30 and 45 minutes | Week 1 of teaching | Ruby |
| Drill bank: every drill Ruby uses, named, with the level it belongs to and the fault it fixes | Add one drill after every lesson | Ruby |
| 8-week milestone map per level (what a swimmer should be able to do at week 2, 4, 6, 8) | Before the first Progress Programme | Ruby |
| Progression rules: when a swimmer moves up a level | Before the first programme ends | Ruby |
| Parent communication: booking confirmation, week 4 note, week 8 report, renewal message, leaver message (templates in `06`) | Before the first block | Chris |
| Poolside routines: arrival, register, parent seating, changing, medical check, incident handling | Before the first lesson at each venue | Chris and Ruby |
| Video analysis protocol for Stroke Clinics: angles, what is measured, the take-home drill plan format | Before the first clinic | Ruby |
| Coach onboarding checklist and observation form | Stage 2 | Ruby |

### RW Method: template outline (v0.1)

```
RW METHOD  (version, date, owner)

1. Purpose and promise
   1.1 What a parent is promised (progress they can see, in writing)
   1.2 What we never do (large groups, unwritten progress, hype)

2. Levels
   2.1 Splash (water confidence, ages 4 to 6): entry criteria, 8 skills, exit criteria
   2.2 Stroke (technique, ages 6 to 10): entry, 8 skills, exit
   2.3 Sprint (club-ready, ages 9 to 14): entry, 8 skills, exit
   2.4 Adults and improvers (later)

3. Assessment
   3.1 The 20-minute taster script
   3.2 The assessment sheet and how to score it
   3.3 The 5-minute parent conversation and what to recommend

4. Lesson design
   4.1 30-minute 1:1 structure
   4.2 30-minute 2:1 structure
   4.3 45-minute group of 4 structure
   4.4 60-minute stroke clinic structure
   4.5 Holiday intensive 5-day arc

5. Drill bank (by level, by fault)

6. Progress and reporting
   6.1 8-week milestone maps
   6.2 Week 4 note template
   6.3 Week 8 report and certificate
   6.4 Move-up rules

7. Poolside standards
   7.1 Safety routine and ratios
   7.2 Safeguarding practice (never alone with a child, changing rooms, photos)
   7.3 Behaviour and the "one warning" rule
   7.4 Incident handling

8. Parent experience
   8.1 Communication cadence and tone
   8.2 Renewal at week 5
   8.3 Handling a leaver

9. Coaches
   9.1 Minimum standards
   9.2 Onboarding path and observation form
   9.3 Quality checks (parent feedback, renewal rate per coach)

Appendix: forms (from 06-templates.md)
```

Keep it in one shared folder. Version it. Every change is dated. This is the asset.

## 7. The community angle (keep it separate)

There is a genuine community story: children in the district who never learn to swim because lessons are unaffordable, school swimming that does not get every child to 25 metres, and a local competitive swimmer wanting to give something back. It is a good story and it will come up.

**Keep it separate from the trading business for now, because:**

- A Community Interest Company (CIC) has an asset lock and a community-benefit test; it cannot later be sold or take ordinary investment in the way a Ltd can. Mixing the two muddles the investor story in section 8.
- A CIC or charity partnership needs its own governance, accounts and (for grants) a track record. That is Chris's admin time, which is the scarcest resource in Stage 1.
- The business needs to prove it can pay for itself before it gives anything away.

**What you can do now, at little cost and honestly:** offer 2 free taster places a term to a local primary school for children the school nominates; state it plainly on the website ("Every term, two free places for children a local school tells us need them"). It costs two lane slots.

**When to revisit:** Stage 3, or earlier if one of these appears: a school or council asking for a school-swimming top-up programme; a grant (Sport England, Swim England, a district council community fund, a local trust) that needs a not-for-profit vehicle; a sponsor who wants a community programme to attach a name to. At that point the structure is usually **RW Swim Academy Ltd (trading) plus a separate RW Swim Foundation CIC (community)**, with the Ltd donating coach hours at cost.

## 9. Risks (the honest list)

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | What we do about it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public leisure centres refuse lane hire to a private teacher at after-school times (they run their own lesson programmes and see us as competition) | High | High | Approach school pools, hotel pools and home pools first; ask leisure centres for off-peak and Saturday early slots; never depend on one venue **[CONFIRM each venue's policy]** |

| Ruby's age used against her by a competitor or a sceptical parent | Medium | Low | Lead with qualifications, DBS, insurance and written progress; parents on poolside see the manner and stop worrying |
