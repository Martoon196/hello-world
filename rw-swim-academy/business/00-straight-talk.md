## 1. The business, honestly

RW Swim Academy is not a swim school. Not yet. It is **one very good, very marketable coach with about 15 to 20 teaching hours a week**, because Ruby's own training comes first and always will. That is the ceiling, and it changes everything:

- You are selling a **scarce, premium** thing, not a volume thing. Price accordingly. The council pool sells volume; you can't and shouldn't.
- The business model is **waiting list + renewals**. Every slot Ruby has should be booked term after term by the same families. New customers fill gaps, they aren't the engine.
- The job in year one is to **fill Ruby**, collect proof, and write down how she does it. Not to build a company around her.

If Ruby is full at premium prices with a waiting list by Easter 2027, you have a business. If she isn't, no amount of branding, investors or industrial units will fix it. That is the test, and it costs almost nothing to run.

## 3. What the group missed

1. **Pool access is the whole game, not the logo.** In Sevenoaks the constraint is lane hire at sensible hours (4pm to 7pm weekdays, Saturday mornings). Every leisure centre wants those hours for its own lessons. **The single most important task this month is getting written lane-hire agreements at two venues.** If that fails, the model changes (see next point). Email templates are in `06`, section J.

2. **Home pools are the best product you have.** The Weald is full of private pools. A lesson at the family's own pool has zero lane cost, no changing-room chaos, a wealthy customer who values convenience, and a competitor (SwimFitz) already proving people pay for it. At £55 to £60 a lesson the margin is roughly three times a hired-lane lesson. It's now on the website as "Lessons at your pool". Push it hard from May to September. Ruby needs a car and a pool safety checklist (in `06`).

3. **Price for what Ruby is, not what the leisure centre charges.** A county finalist who is still racing, teaching one to one, is worth more than £30 an hour. The prices in `BRIEF.md` are deliberately at the top of the local range. Do not discount to win the first customers; use the founding-member 10% as the *only* discount, and only for the first 30.

4. **The sale happens at the school gate and in Facebook groups, not on the website.** The website's job is to look credible and take the booking. The leads come from three or four Sevenoaks parent Facebook groups, PTAs, and word of mouth from the first 10 families. Chris's job in the first 90 days is to be the person who gets Ruby into those conversations. See `marketing/ads-and-outreach/02-local-outreach.md`.

5. **Written progress notes are the retention engine.** Parents renew when they can see progress. A one-paragraph note at week 4 and week 6, with skills ticked, is worth more than any ad spend. It is built into the offer for that reason. Ruby must actually do it, every block, without fail.

6. **Ruby's competition calendar is a business risk. Plan it.** Galas, camps and taper weeks are known months ahead. They go in the Google Calendar as busy time *before* the booking system opens, so no parent ever has a lesson cancelled for a gala. That is the one thing that would kill trust fastest.

7. **Keep it a sole trader for the first six months.** Simple, cheap, and the tax is fine at these numbers. Go Ltd when revenue passes roughly £30k a year or when a second coach is hired. Details in `03` section 9 and `04`.

## 4. The five numbers to watch

| Number | Target by end of March 2027 |
|---|---|
| Active paying swimmers | 30 |
| Taster to block conversion | 50% or better |
| Block renewal rate | 75% or better |
| Ruby's booked teaching hours per week | 12+ (out of about 15 available) |
| Waiting list | 15+ |

Hit four of the five and you move to Stage 2 with confidence. Miss three of the five and you have learned something cheaply. Either way you'll know, and nobody will have bought an industrial unit.
